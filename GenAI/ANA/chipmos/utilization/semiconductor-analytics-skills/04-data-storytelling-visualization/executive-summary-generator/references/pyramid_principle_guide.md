# Pyramid Principle for Utilization Reports

1. **Answer first** - one sentence of about 30 words with the headline number in plain words and the decision. Example (from the Sep-Oct 2026 summary): "On average 91% of front test positions and 96% of rear positions were able to test; three groups of front positions are still out of service, so we recommend inspecting them first."
2. **Three key lines** - where the loss is, whether it is current or history, and whether there is a trend. Use what is happening **now** (the recency view), not only the month average: a group that was fixed is history, a group that just broke is news.
3. **Evidence** - one number each, taken from the analysis document, with a range where one exists.
4. **Limits** - what is not measured (OEE needs run/idle time, output, good-unit and cycle-time data), the plausible range, and any cause that is only a hypothesis.
5. **Ask** - action, owner, date.

Rules
- One page, about 300 words at most; method detail goes in the analysis document.
- Say "recover" only for a repair that is known to restore the positions; otherwise say "cost" or "lost".
- Report capacity in availability points. Money and units per hour stay out until the deferred impact skill has real business parameters.
- Avoid project jargon (chronic, cluster, near-full, read failure, bootstrap); `scripts/exec_summary_check.py` flags it.
