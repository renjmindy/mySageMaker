01 Singapore team's findings — what we heard

Three independent observations from the SingHealth and MOH-Holdings teams, all consistent with each other and pointing at the same underlying bug.

1.1 Chin De Zhi — the inversion observation

"Prelim testing using the new links did not produce the expected results. I have negative Theta values for MQ and SQ item banks when the best response options are chosen and positive Theta values when the worst options are chosen." — Chin De Zhi (SGH), email of 12 May 2026, 5:08 PM

This is the **bug we reproduced exactly today**. See §03 and §04.

1.2 Kwan Yu Heng — the "static questionnaire" observation

"We tested the link — for some reason the SHAWS CAT always give the same 8 qns for all — as if its a static questionnaire. There is no algorithm or it seem the stopping rules that doesn't work. For eg we try to fill in the last option for everyone — it is the same result. We try to fill in between random options — it is also the same 8 qns. There is no difference in using a CAT or not." — Kwan Yu Heng (MOHH), 12 May 2026, 3:40 PM

Two observations are bundled here. We addressed both in today's testing:

  • "Same 8 questions for all" — partially explained by the engine's randomesque top-N selection rule combined with a small effective pool. Different test sessions did produce different first items in our runs today (PQ31 in one, PQ50 in another), confirming the randomize-first feature is operating. But the second-and-onwards picks draw from a relatively narrow pool, so the visible variability across patients is lower than a fully-adaptive instrument would show.
  • "No difference between best and worst responses" — this is the inversion bug in action. Because the engine computes θ̂ in the wrong direction for negative-α items, the response-pattern effect on item selection is muted. The two profiles do produce different item sequences (we observed this today), but the difference is smaller than expected because θ̂ is unstable.

1.3 Silvana Choo Xinyi — the adaptive-logic observation

"Fixed number of questions presented regardless of responses. In both attempts, exactly 8 questions were presented for each domain regardless of how I responded. Question order does not appear to follow adaptive logic. The sequence in which questions were presented does not appear to reflect adaptive selection. In a correctly functioning CAT, the tool should recognise that a respondent who cannot perform a less demanding activity would not need to be asked about a more demanding one." — Silvana Choo Xinyi (SGH), 12 May 2026

Silvana's clinical intuition is exactly right — a CAT-IRT engine should walk down the difficulty ladder when a respondent reports inability. The fact that this isn't visibly happening in your testing is a symptom of the same root cause: θ̂ is being computed in the wrong direction for negative-α items, so the engine's MFI selection at the (wrong) θ̂ doesn't reflect the patient's actual ability level.

The three observations are the same bug, manifesting differently. De Zhi sees it as score-direction inversion. Yu Heng sees it as low session-to-session variability. Silvana sees it as item-selection not respecting clinical common sense. All three are downstream symptoms of the engine treating raw negative-α items without sign normalisation.

02 How we tested today

To validate your team's concerns rigorously and produce evidence usable by both psychometric and engineering teams, we ran two complementary CAT sessions. The methodology mirrors your team's worst-case Consistency-2 protocol, plus its mirror — a Consistency-1 (best-case) profile.

2.1 Two consistency profiles, run on the same form

ProfileResponse ruleExpected θ̂ directionWhy this design
Worst-case (Consistency-2) For every item, select the lowest-trait response option ("Unable to do" / "Never" / "Not at all") Negative for every section Reproduces the protocol from your 81-cell QA matrix. Drives θ̂ to the low-trait end
Best-case (Consistency-1) For every item, select the highest-trait response option ("Without any difficulty" / "Always") Positive for every section The mirror profile. With a correctly-functioning engine, results should be ~mirror reflections of the worst-case run across θ = 0

2.2 Why the mirror design is diagnostic

A correctly-functioning CAT engine produces approximately mirror-symmetric θ̂ values for the two profiles. A bugged engine that inverts direction will produce the same sign for both profiles, or a cross-inverted pattern (worst-case positive, best-case negative). The two-run design produces an unambiguous direction signal that a single run cannot give.

2.3 What was held constant

  • Environment — Dev 2 (https://internal-dev-2-patient.zedoc.dev/)
  • Form — the same patient-facing form your team is currently testing against
  • Calibration — your unmodified SHAW-CAT calibration as deployed in that form (α, β unchanged)
  • Engine — the deployed CAT engine version for that form

Only the response pattern differed between the two runs. Any difference in θ̂ output therefore reflects the engine's response-to-θ̂ mapping behaviour, not a configuration difference.

4.1 Two-run comparison — the mirror evidence

θ̂ by section, both profiles

The SQ result is the headline. Worst-case responses → θ̂ = +4.61. Best-case responses → θ̂ = −4.70. These are **mirror reflections across zero** — the engine is computing θ̂ in the literal opposite direction of what the responses indicate. A perfectly-engineered bug.

4.2 What this proves

  • The bug is systematic, not a one-off measurement artefact. Two completely independent response patterns produce mirror-symmetric inverted results.
  • The bug affects only direction, not magnitude. The absolute value of θ̂ is approximately right (~4.6 in both cases — appropriate for an extreme respondent profile). Only the sign is flipped.
  • PQ is largely unaffected because the PQ items administered are dominated by high-α positive-α items (e.g., PQ42 marathon at α=5.02). Positive-α items work correctly in both engine states. PQ working should NOT be taken as evidence the engine works overall.
  • MQ shows a moderate bug signal — negative θ̂ in both runs — because random-first picks different starting items in the two runs, producing different α-sign mixes in the administered set. The bug is present, but more subtle than in SQ.

05 The bug mechanism — why this is happening

The math: when the engine processes a calibration with α < 0 but treats α directly (without sign normalisation), the GRM probability function reverses its monotonicity in θ. The MLE estimator then maximises likelihood in the opposite direction of what the response coding indicates.

5.1 The 2PL cumulative function

For a single threshold β with discrimination α:

2PL cumulative P*(θ; α, β) = σ(α · (θ − β)),   σ(x) = (1 + e−x)−1

The slope direction is controlled by the sign of α. If α > 0, P* increases as θ rises. If α < 0, P* increases as θ falls. Without normalisation, the engine processes negative-α items with the inverted slope.

5.2 Interactive demonstration — drag the slider

α = −1.12 (live SQ calibration). P*(θ) decreases in θ — the cumulative probability of endorsing higher categories falls as the latent trait rises. The MLE for a high-category response pattern lands on the wrong side of zero. This is the bug.

5.3 Why each section behaves differently

Each section's behaviour depends on the α² balance of items administered. We use the squared α (Fisher information weight) because that's what determines the magnitude of each item's pull on θ̂:

SectionPositive-α α² (items pull correct direction)Negative-α α² (items pull wrong direction)Dominant directionObserved behaviour
PQ — 5 items administered ~45.9 (PQ42 α=5.02 dominates) ~1.3 Positive-α overwhelmingly dominates (~35×) Bug invisible — direction looks correct
MQ — 8 items administered, depends on which items 1.5 to 4.8 (varies) 2.9 to 3.4 (varies) Either can dominate depending on random-first pick Bug partially visible — magnitude inflated, direction sometimes wrong
SQ — 8 items administered ~0.4 (only one positive-α item in pool) ~4.4 (7 negative-α items) Negative-α overwhelmingly dominates (~11×) Bug fully visible — direction fully inverted

This α² balance explanation is consistent across both test runs and explains why different patients see different magnitudes of the bug.

06 Proposed path forward — the recommended fix

A single, narrowly-scoped change at the calibration-load layer of the engine. No new flags, no new section-level configuration, no θ × −1 post-estimator multiplication. Empirical evidence from a separate Dev 2 test environment earlier today (described in §07) confirms this approach produces correct θ̂ for the SHAW-CAT instrument.

6.1 Statement of the fix

Inside getParamsFromConfig (the function in utilities.js that translates your calibration JSON into the internal parameter map the estimator uses), apply a pre-pass per item:

Normalisation αj* = |αj|,   βj,k* = βj,k  (k = 1, …, Kj)

Every item's α is replaced with its absolute value at load time. β values are left unchanged. The calibration JSON is treated as read-only; the normalisation is in-memory only.

6.2 Why this is mathematically correct for SHAW-CAT

Under the GRM, P*(θ; −α, β) = 1 − P*(θ; α, β) by the logistic identity σ(−x) = 1 − σ(x). Negating α reflects the cumulative curve across the P = 0.5 line; this is equivalent (in MLE terms) to reversing the response codes for the item. For SHAW-CAT items, the response codes are calibrated so that high codes = high trait — therefore negating α is the correct sign convention. Each item gets the same treatment based on its own α sign; no manual flagging is required.

Crucially: the SHAW-CAT items with α < 0 in your calibration are all positively framed ("I have a good relationship with my family", "I am able to accept who I am", "I feel loved and cared for"). The negative α is therefore a calibration-software sign-convention artefact, not a semantic reversal of the item meaning. The simple α-flip recovers the intended scoring direction. No θ-level inversion is needed.

6.3 What gets preserved

Item information

Per-item Fisher information I(θ) = α²·P·(1−P) depends only on α². Since |α|² = α², the information ranking is unchanged. The CAT engine's MFI selection picks the same items before and after the fix.

Item-level β values

β coefficients are untouched. Your calibration's threshold placements remain exactly as your team published.

Calibration file integrity

The normalisation is in-memory only. Your sq_config.json and equivalent files stay unmodified on disk — preserving provenance and avoiding manual data updates per your client request.

07 Why this option, not the alternatives

Today's internal Slack discussion between Slava and Mindy converged on the same conclusion via different paths. For transparency, here are the alternative approaches considered and why each was set aside.

ApproachDescriptionVerdict
Auto-normalise α at config load Recommended Detect α < 0 and replace with |α| at calibration-load time, per item. No section-level configuration. Adopt. Empirically validated this morning on a separate Dev 2 environment (SQ θ̂ = −4.41 for worst-case responses — correct direction). Mathematically equivalent to the manual workaround your team has used during testing.
Multiply θ̂ by −1 after estimation Run the estimator with raw α (or after α-flip), then multiply the final θ̂ by −1. Mathematically equivalent to α-flip when applied alone, but dangerous to apply on top of α-flip — would double-invert and re-introduce the bug. Not needed if α-flip is implemented at config load.
Per-section invert_theta: true flag Manually flag sections (SQ, MQ) as "wellness-positive" and multiply θ̂ by −1 only for flagged sections. Reject. Adds configuration burden, doesn't auto-detect new banks, and the per-item α-flip already provides the same logical effect without requiring a section-level decision.

Per the client request, calibration files should not be modified. Also creates provenance issues — the team's published α values diverge from the engine's operative α values.

08 Implementation plan & timeline

8.1 What needs to happen

  1. Confirm the diagnostic. Engineering inspects the calibration-load path for the deployed SHAW-CAT form and confirms whether the existing getParamsFromConfig α-flip code is being called for this form's irtConfigFormula → variableValue resolution chain. Today's tests strongly indicate it isn't.
  2. Deploy the fix to the form's code path. If the α-flip code is not running for the form, route the calibration load through it. If the code is running but bypassed by a subsequent layer, identify and remove the bypass.
  3. Add belt-and-braces guards. Post-α-flip, assert β is non-decreasing (per Mindy's earlier review). Throw a named error if the contract is violated. This guards against future calibrations shipped with descending β.
  4. Add 4 unit tests covering: (i) negative-α normalisation per item, (ii) θ̂-equivalence between (α, β) and (−α, β) parametrisations, (iii) information-invariance under α-sign, (iv) contract assertion on β monotonicity.
  5. Re-run the two-profile validation (today's worst-case + best-case protocols) on the fixed form. Pass criteria: SQ θ̂ ≈ −4 to −5 for worst-case, ≈ +4 to +5 for best-case (mirror symmetric, correct direction).

09 Discussion points for tonight's meeting

Four questions we'd value alignment on during tonight's discussion. None requires a decision in the meeting if it isn't ready — these are framings to help the conversation converge.

1. Do we agree on the diagnosis?

The two-run mirror evidence (SQ θ̂ = +4.61 worst-case, −4.70 best-case) and the cross-environment comparison (same form configuration on a separate Dev 2 deployment producing the correct −4.41 with α-flip operating) together point at a single diagnosis: the per-item α-normalisation is not being applied in the calibration-load path of the form your team is currently testing. Is this diagnosis your psychometric team's read as well?

2. Do we agree on the proposed fix?

Per-item auto-flip of α at calibration load time (Slava's existing approach), no additional θ-level inversion, no section-level flags. Mathematically equivalent to the manual workaround your team has been using during testing, and to Mindy's auto-detect-by-α-sign proposal. Any psychometric concerns from your side?

3. Randomesque pool size — top-5, top-3, or weighted?

Yu Heng and Silvana's observations about non-adaptive behaviour have a second component independent of the α-sign bug: the top-5 randomesque selection introduces noise into MFI ordering. Mindy's earlier review suggested reducing to top-2 or 3, or implementing information-weighting (e.g., #1 item gets 70% probability). What's your team's preference for the production deployment — keep top-5, narrow to top-3, or move to weighted selection?

4. SE-threshold and maxItems calibration

With the bug operating, today's MQ section terminated at 8 items with SE = 0.84 (well above the 0.30 threshold). Once the fix lands, we'd expect MQ to terminate earlier and within-threshold for typical profiles. Should we plan a follow-up session to verify the SE ≤ 0.30 threshold is achievable within 8 items across the three banks after the fix is live? If not, options are to widen to 10 items or relax the threshold to 0.40.
